# SharpLsp messaging

## Fundamental message

**Free. Open source. No lock-in.**

All three are fundamental messaging items. **“No lock-in” is mandatory and must recur across SharpLsp's public messaging.** “Free” and “open source” do not replace it: say “No lock-in” explicitly.

SharpLsp brings F# and C# development to the editor you choose. Its language engine is open source and editor-agnostic, with no proprietary dependencies or per-seat licensing requirement for SharpLsp.

## Required use

- **Thumbnails and promotional graphics:** display “FREE.”, “OPEN SOURCE.” and “NO LOCK-IN.” as three distinct, readable statements.
- **YouTube descriptions and channel copy:** include “Free. Open source. No lock-in.” near the beginning. Keep the feature or video topic clear alongside it.
- **Website, release announcements and campaign copy:** repeat “No lock-in” in the main value proposition and reinforce it where editor choice or licensing is discussed.
- **Titles and headings:** use “No lock-in” when it fits naturally; every title need not contain the entire slogan.

Repeat the message across assets and releases. Do not leave it implicit in a source-code link or mention it only once in a long document.

## Meaning and claim boundaries

“No lock-in” means developers can choose their editor and use SharpLsp's open-source language engine without a proprietary SharpLsp license. Explain that benefit in concrete terms: editor choice, open protocols and an open-source engine.

Keep claims specific to SharpLsp. Do not imply every editor, SDK, platform or third-party integration is free, or that every planned integration already ships. F# remains a first-class citizen alongside C#.

> Free. Open source. No lock-in.

Product line:
